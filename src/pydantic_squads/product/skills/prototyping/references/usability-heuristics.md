# Usability heuristics

Check every screen against these before delivering (after Nielsen's ten):

- **Visibility of system status** — the user always knows what is happening:
  loading indicators, progress through multi-step flows, confirmation after
  an action.
- **Match with the real world** — use the users' words, not internal jargon
  or database field names. HX's findings tell you which words they use.
- **User control and freedom** — every step has a clear way back or out;
  destructive-looking actions can be undone or confirmed.
- **Consistency and standards** — the same action looks and behaves the
  same everywhere; follow platform conventions and the design system.
- **Error prevention** — constrain inputs, give sensible defaults, and
  disable actions that cannot succeed yet.
- **Recognition over recall** — show options instead of making users
  remember them; keep context visible across steps.
- **Flexibility and efficiency** — shortcuts for frequent actions without
  getting in a first-time user's way.
- **Minimalist design** — every element earns its place; secondary
  information goes one tap deeper.
- **Help users recover from errors** — error messages say what went wrong,
  in plain language, and how to fix it, next to where it happened.
- **Help and documentation** — needed help is contextual and short, not a
  separate manual.
